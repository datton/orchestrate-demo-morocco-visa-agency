Before you fly: six steps

  1. Check whether your passport needs a visa

    UK passport holders (full British citizen passports) can visit Morocco without a visa for up to 90 days for tourism. For other purposes, check with the Moroccan consulate. For any other nationality, check whether your passport needs a visa with the Moroccan embassy or consulate.

  2. Check your passport's validity

    For UK travellers, the passport must expire at least 3 months after the arrival date. Other embassies and guides give different figures, so most travellers should plan on at least 3 to 6 months of validity and confirm the exact rule with their embassy.

  3. If you need an e-visa, use the official portal

    Morocco runs an online e-visa for eligible nationalities. Fees, validity and processing times differ between sources, so we do not quote them here: check the official portal or the Moroccan consulate for the current rules.

  4. Gather your supporting papers

    Many travellers carry proof of accommodation, a return or onward ticket and proof of funds. Embassy document lists were not available to us on 20.11.2026, so ask your embassy or consulate which papers they require for your case.

  5. Check health and arrival forms

    A polio vaccination certificate is required if you arrive from a polio-affected country. Whether an arrival health form is needed has been reported differently: check with the embassy or official portal before you travel.

  6. Prepare cash and valuables

    Foreign currency of 100,000 MAD or more must be declared on entry or exit. Never post your passport to or through Morocco: it is illegal and it can be confiscated.

At the border

  • Make sure your passport is stamped on entry. Travellers without a stamp have had trouble leaving.
  • For a UK passport the tourism limit is 90 days. Staying longer needs an extension request at a Moroccan police station.
  • Declare currency of 100,000 MAD or more.
