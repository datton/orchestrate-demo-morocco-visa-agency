Staying longer in Morocco

Visa-free visits are limited in length. This guide covers what the UK government says about longer stays, what to prepare, and where to confirm the rules for your own passport. We are a paperwork-help agency, not the Moroccan government or an embassy, and the decision is always Morocco's.

The rule in short

UK passport holders (full British citizen passports) can visit Morocco without a visa for up to 90 days for tourism. Stays beyond 90 days need an extension request at a Moroccan police station. For other purposes, check with the Moroccan consulate.

Not a UK passport holder? Check whether your passport needs a visa, and how long you may stay, with the Moroccan embassy or consulate.

Steps

  1. Get the entry stamp. Make sure your passport is stamped when you enter. Travellers without a stamp have had trouble leaving.
  2. Count your days. Note your arrival date and work out the day your 90 days end, well before it arrives.
  3. Check your passport. For UK travellers it must expire at least 3 months after your arrival date. Other sources suggest 3 to 6 months for other passports: check with your embassy.
  4. Go to a police station. Ask for an extension before the 90 days run out. Which documents and fees they want: check with the embassy or the police station, we do not state them here.
  5. Keep your paperwork. Keep the stamped passport and anything the police give you until you have left.

What to prepare

  • Your passport, with a clear entry stamp and enough validity left.
  • Your arrival date and the date your 90 days end.
  • Questions for the police station or consulate on documents, since we have not confirmed a document list.

Overstay and exit risks

The UK government warns that travellers without an entry stamp have had trouble leaving. Staying past your permitted time without an extension is a risk to your exit. We do not state penalties here: check with the Moroccan consulate.

Other things to know

  • Foreign currency of 100,000 MAD or more must be declared on entry or exit.
  • Never post your passport to or through Morocco: it is illegal and it will be confiscated.
